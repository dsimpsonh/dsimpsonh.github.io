Research

Three questions run through the work.

How a machine should rank people, when it should decline, and what record it owes the people it ranks. The questions come out of building Aletheai; the answers are written up in the patents, the essays and the methods.

When should a machine decline to answer?

Ranking only where the evidence supports it, and recording the refusal, so a person can see where the system could not see.

How do you govern what an AI agent does without reading its mind?

Gate the action, not the reasoning: evidence the agent did not write, checked by a gate it cannot reach.

What does it take for a decision to be defensible afterwards?

A record a person can open: what was known, what was inferred, and why this item came first.

Biography

Design, behaviour and AI.

Diana Simpson-Hernandez works where design, behaviour and AI meet: on systems that help people make decisions they can defend afterwards.

She trained in product design at Central Saint Martins and the Royal College of Art, where her MA dissertation, Modern Mythopoeia (2012), argued that the stories a culture tells itself decide which futures get built, and that designers can work on those stories on purpose. The speculative practice turned practical. Glass Lab made surface products from the commercial glass waste of London businesses, and at Shake Your Power she co-designed SPARK, the first energy-generating musical instrument, which was granted a US patent, won two London Design Award Golds and was piloted in schools in the UK, the US and India.

She then spent a decade in product and service design and in teaching. She was Senior Lecturer in Product Design at Nottingham Trent University, and later led the digital design team and product strategy at Slimming World, one of the UK's largest behaviour-change platforms, where the work was keeping more than 250,000 members engaged with a health service week after week.

In December 2024 she founded Aletheai to build the decision layer she kept finding missing: an engine that ranks who deserves attention by the evidence, explains each ranking in plain English, and declines to rank where the evidence runs out. She has filed six patent applications, on the engine and on the governance of AI agents' actions, and ran a feasibility study with the STFC Hartree Centre on real human-behaviour data.

She teaches AI-native product building to MA students at IED Madrid every year, mentors founders at London College of Communication (UAL), and publishes her methods in the open. She is a Fellow of the Royal Society of Arts and an Associate Fellow of the Higher Education Academy, and works in English and Spanish.
